# Signature extension X2: proposal experiments

Two proposal strategies are A/B tested in GigSim with a significance test on reply rate. All numbers below are
from `evals/results/x2_experiment.json` (runner `evals/runners/x2_experiment.py`).

## Design

- Strategy A: `agent/prompts/writer.md`, evidence first (lead with the closest past project and its outcome).
- Strategy B: `agent/prompts/writer_b.md`, outcome and timeline first (lead with the deliverable, date and price).
- The same pool of bid jobs (jobs the graph decides to bid on, scam and injected jobs excluded) is ordered by a
  hash, and each job gets exactly one strategy by the parity of `sha256("x2:" + job_id)`. Assignment is
  therefore random with respect to job content and reproducible.
- Writer model: `groq/qwen/qwen3.8-27b` (profile `open_hosted`), retriever V2 hybrid for both arms.
- Outcome: the GigSim simulated reply (binary) after the draft is auto approved in process.
- Test: two proportion z test on reply rate, Fisher exact test as a small sample check, 95 percent confidence
  interval on the difference (scipy).
- Planned n was 60 jobs, reduced to 40 to fit the Groq free tier (1,000 requests per day). 39 proposals completed.

## Result

| Arm | Strategy | n | Replies | Reply rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | evidence first | 19 | 5 | 0.263 |
| B | outcome and timeline first | 20 | 3 | 0.150 |

Difference B minus A is -0.113, 95 percent CI -0.366 to +0.139, z = -0.87, two sided p = 0.38, Fisher exact
p = 0.45. **Not significant.** At this n the test could only detect a difference of about 0.36 (80 percent
power), so a real difference of up to a third could have been missed. The simulator's own reply probability
(a diagnostic that does not exist in the real world) was 0.312 for A and 0.286 for B, p = 0.42, which agrees
with the primary result: no detectable difference.

## What this says

- There is no evidence that either strategy is better. A is ahead by 11 points but that is well inside the noise.
- The experiment is under powered by design: GigSim draws replies from a pseudo random formula per proposal id,
  so detecting a 10 point difference around a 20 percent reply rate would need roughly 250 proposals per arm (standard two proportion power calculation, not measured here).
- Strategy A stays the default because nothing here justifies a change. Strategy B stays available through the
  `writer_strategy` setting for a larger test.

## Limits

- GigSim replies are generated by our own simulator, so a difference would show the simulator's reply model,
  not real client behaviour.
- Retrieval is held fixed, so only the writing strategy varies.
- One run per job, temperature 0.2, one model.
